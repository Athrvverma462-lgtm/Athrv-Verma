Enter text for sequence 1: The Library on the Hill
High on a blustery cliff overlooking the ocean stood an ancient stone library that held only one 
copy of every book ever forgotten by the world. The librarian, an old gentleman named Arthur, spent 
his days organizing shelves that stretched up toward vaulted wooden ceilings.

One evening, a high school boy named Leo snuck into the library to take shelter from a sudden downpour. 
Curious, Leo wandered through the aisles until he pulled a small, dusty leather notebook off a shelf.

There was no title on the cover. Inside, every page was filled with hand-drawn sketches of strange 
inventions, starry skies, and handwritten thoughts in messy blue ink.

"Ah, you found Master Daniel's journal," Arthur's calm voice echoed from down the aisle.

Leo startled, nearly dropping the book. "Sorry! I was just getting out of the rain. Who was Daniel?"

"An inventor from eighty years ago," Arthur said, stepping closer with a warm lantern. "He spent his 
entire life designing flying machines, water purifiers, and automated plows. But he never published his 
work, and when he passed away, his attic was cleared out."

Leo flipped to a page with a detailed drawing of a solar-powered water collector. "These ideas are 
brilliant! Why did nobody ever build them?"

"Because Daniel was afraid," Arthur said gently. "He was terrified that people would laugh at his ideas, 
so he kept them all in that single notebook."

Leo looked down at the yellowed pages, suddenly feeling a knot in his stomach. He remembered the 
notebooks stacked under his own bed at home, full of stories and drawings he had never shown a single 
soul out of fear of being judged.

"It seems sad," Leo whispered. "All these great ideas, sitting in the dark forever."

"They don't have to stay in the dark," Arthur said, handing Leo a pencil and a blank notepad. "The 
library collects what the world forgets, but anyone who walks in here is free to carry a piece of it 
back out."

That night, as the rain tapped against the high glass windows, Leo sat at a heavy wooden table and began 
copying the diagrams, ready to bring them into the light.
